The **Court of Arbitration for Sport** will announce its final decision on **Tuesday, November 24**, in the long-running dispute over the **2025 Africa Cup of Nations final** between Senegal and Morocco. The verdict, due Tuesday afternoon, will settle which nation is officially recognised as continental champion after months of litigation.

CAS said Monday that it had notified all parties of the decision date. The ruling follows a closed-door hearing at the court's Lausanne headquarters on **October 8**.

The case stems from the final played in Rabat on **January 18, 2026**, when Senegal beat hosts Morocco **1-0 after extra time**. Pape Gueye scored the winning goal. The match was disrupted when Senegal's players briefly left the field to protest a late penalty awarded to Morocco. Play resumed after about 15 minutes, and Morocco's Brahim Diaz had his Panenka attempt saved by Senegal goalkeeper Edouard Mendy. Senegal then scored in extra time through Gueye and appeared to have won its second AFCON title.

On **March 17**, however, CAF's Appeal Board overturned the result. It ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match by leaving the pitch without authorisation and awarded Morocco a **3-0 win** under Articles 82 and 84 of the competition regulations.

The Senegalese Football Federation appealed to CAS on **March 25**, asking the court to overturn CAF's decision and restore Senegal as champion. Morocco's football federation is defending CAF's ruling and wants to keep the trophy.
